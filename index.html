ℹ️ What do these numbers mean?
  • In deficit — the share of students whose monthly expenses exceed their monthly resources (income + financial aid), i.e. savings < 0. These students run out of money before the month ends. Here it is 70.5% of students.
  • Gini (spending) — a measure of inequality of monthly spending across students, borrowed from economics. 0 = everyone spends exactly the same; 1 = extreme inequality (one person spends everything). Our value (~0.08) is very low → spending is spread quite evenly, which is expected for a synthetic dataset.
  • Currency — the dataset does not specify a currency. All amounts (income, expenses, savings) are generic monetary units (commonly read as US dollars). Only the relative magnitudes matter, not the absolute unit.

ℹ️ How to read this tab — metrics & models
  • The task: predict whether a student is in deficit (financial_stress = savings < 0) from their spending, income and demographics. The data is split 75% train / 25% test.
  • cv_auc — mean ROC-AUC over 5-fold cross-validation (the data is split into 5 parts and the model is trained/tested 5 times). ROC-AUC measures how well the model separates the two classes: 1.0 = perfect, 0.5 = random guessing. Averaging over folds makes the estimate robust.
  • test_auc — ROC-AUC on the held-out 25% test set the model never saw during training.
  • test_acc — accuracy on the test set: the fraction of students whose deficit / no-deficit status was predicted correctly (e.g. 0.90 = 90% correct).
  • LogReg (Logistic Regression) — a simple linear model that estimates the probability of deficit; fast and interpretable.
  • RandomForest — an ensemble of many decision trees that vote; captures non-linear patterns and provides feature importance.
  • GradientBoosting — decision trees built one after another, each correcting the previous one's errors; often the most accurate.
  • SVM (Support Vector Machine) — finds the boundary that best separates the two classes; works well in high-dimensional spaces.
  • KNN (k-Nearest Neighbours) — classifies each student by the majority label of the most similar students around it.

Statistical hypothesis testing

ℹ️ What does “null” mean here?
  • Each test starts from a null hypothesis (H₀) of “no effect / no relationship.” The p_value is the probability of seeing this result if H₀ were true.
  • If p < 0.05 → the effect is significant (we reject H₀). If p ≥ 0.05 → null (no effect): we cannot conclude any real difference/relationship.
  • Example: ANOVA monthly_expenses ~ major is null → average spending does not differ significantly between majors. Chi-square is “independent” → payment method does not depend on major.
  • Why everything is null: the dataset is synthetically generated, so the variables are unrelated by design (max raw correlation ≈ 0.075). Reporting these null results honestly is itself a valid statistical-inference finding.
